# Spain Pick-and-Roll (Action Reference)

**One-line:** Ball-screen + a back-screen on the dropping big's defender. The roller is unguarded.

## Diagram (text)
```
       PG (with ball)
       |
   5 (ball-screen) → rolls
       |
       ←  4 (back-screens 5's defender)
            then pops to the 3-point line
```

## How it works
1. PG comes off a ball-screen from the 5
2. As the 5's defender drops to defend the roll, the 4 sets a **back-screen** on him
3. The 5 rolls free to the rim
4. The 4 pops out for an open 3 (his defender helped on the back-screen)

## Why it's brutal
Drop coverage is the most common ball-screen defense. Spain attacks the dropping big — the foundation of every drop scheme. There's no clean answer.

## Defense counters
- **Switch the back-screen** — but now your big is on the popping 4 (mismatch)
- **Pre-call "Spain coming" so the dropping big knows to fight through**
- **Top-lock the back-screener** if you can identify him pre-action
- **Switch everything** is the cleanest answer

## When to call it
- ATO out of timeouts
- Late-clock possessions vs drop teams
- Vs traditional (non-switching) bigs

### Verification notes (Claude)
- The Spanish national team / Sergio Scariolo origin is well-supported by the Mundo Deportivo article and Scariolo's own clinic talks. Treat that attribution as solid.
- The defensive counters (switch back-screen, pre-call, top-lock the back-screener, switch everything) are standard NBA scout responses and broadly correct, though "switch everything" only solves Spain if your switching big can both contain the roll and contest the pop — easier said than done.
- The "no clean answer" framing in the file is a common coaching opinion, not a measured fact.
