# Zoom Action

**One-line:** Down-screen → dribble hand-off (DHO). Two screens in one action for a shooter.

## How it works
1. Wing receives a **down-screen** from a big in the lane
2. Wing comes off and immediately receives a **DHO** from the same big at the top of the key
3. Wing is now downhill with momentum, defender scrambling

## Why it works
Defender has to navigate two screens in 1.5 seconds — almost always trails. Forces a help defender to step up, which kicks open weak-side shooter.

## Variations
- **Zoom + ball-screen** — after the DHO, the same big sets a ball-screen
- **Zoom slip** — big slips the second screen if defender pre-switches
- **Reject zoom** — wing refuses the DHO and back-cuts

## Defense counters
- **Switch both screens** (clean answer)
- **Top-lock the wing** so they can't curl
- **Veer-switch** — the on-ball defender switches early to the second screen

### Verification notes (Claude)
- The pin-down + DHO mechanic and the Warriors' heavy use of Zoom under Steve Kerr are widely discussed in basketball media; treat the Warriors attribution as accurate but not exclusive — many NBA and college staffs run Zoom heavily today.
- As noted in `chicago.md`: many sources use "Chicago" and "Zoom" interchangeably. The file's distinction (Chicago = pin to start, Zoom = down-screen to start) is one valid school of terminology, not a universal rule.
- "Veer-switch" terminology is correct and widely used as a counter to Zoom/DHO actions, though some staffs call it "next" or "ice the handoff."
