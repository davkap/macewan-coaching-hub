# Cuts (Concept Reference)

Every cut a coach should know by name.

## Basket cut
- Pass + cut hard to the rim
- The simplest cut — punishes ball-watchers

## Back-cut (back-door)
- When defender overplays the catch, cut behind him to the rim
- The Princeton/5-out staple

## UCLA cut
- Wing passes to top → cuts off a high-post screen to the rim
- Named after John Wooden's UCLA offense
- **Counter:** switch the screen

## Flex cut
- Cut along the baseline off a back-screen from the post
- See `offense/flex.md`

## Iverson cut
- Across the FT line off two elbow screens (Horns alignment)
- See `actions/iverson-cut.md`

## Ghost cut
- Fake a screen, then cut to a different spot (often pop for a 3)
- Used vs aggressive switching

## 45-cut
- Weak-side wing cuts to the slot when the strong-side wing drives
- Maintains spacing on penetration

## Laker cut
- Pass to the post + cut over the top of the post
- Triangle / post-entry staple

## Chin cut
- Pass to the high post + cut TO the elbow → cut to the rim
- Princeton signature

## Screen cuts (after setting a screen)
- **Slip** — cut before defender can react
- **Pop** — pop out for a 3
- **Roll** — roll to the rim
- **Re-screen** — set screen again

## Universal cutting rules
1. **Cut hard or don't cut.** Lazy cuts get help defenders rotating wrong.
2. **Cut to a destination.** Always end at a spacing spot or finish at the rim.
3. **Read your defender.** If he turns his head, back-cut. If he plays high, curl. If he's flat, straight cut.

## Sources & further reading

### Verified citations (this file's content can be checked against)
- [Wikipedia — Princeton offense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_offense) — confirms the back-door cut as the Princeton signature, originating with Pete Carril at Princeton (1967–1996), with chin and basket cuts as core actions in a 4-out alignment.
- [Wikipedia — UCLA High Post Offense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA_High_Post_Offense) — confirms the UCLA cut (wing passes to top, then cuts off a high-post screen to the rim) originated in John Wooden's offense, with Tex Winter triangle relations.
- [Wikipedia — Motion offense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_offense) — describes basket cuts, back-screens, and flare screens as core motion principles, with Bob Knight's Indiana motion offense as the canonical reference.

### Recommended further reading for staff
- Fastmodel Sports Playbank — diagrammed examples of every cut type (UCLA, Iverson, chin, Laker, flex, ghost, 45). Browse https://fastmodelsports.com and search by cut name.
- Pete Carril, *The Smart Take from the Strong* (1997) — Carril's own treatment of back-door reads and high-post passing.
- John Wooden & Steve Jamison, *The Wooden System* — describes the original UCLA high-post action.
- HalfCourtHoops / Coach Daniel YouTube channels — short tactical clips on each cut type.
- Triangle offense literature (Tex Winter) — for the Laker cut and the read-off-the-post system.

### Verification notes (Claude)
- "Iverson cut" is named for Allen Iverson because the Sixers under Larry Brown ran it for him constantly; it is well-documented in clinic literature but the naming convention is informal.
- "Chin cut" / "Chin series" is Princeton terminology, generally credited to Carril.
- "Laker cut" naming is loose — many coaches call any cut over the top of a post catch a "Laker" because of the Showtime Lakers' use; it's not a strictly defined term.
- "45-cut" terminology is regionally inconsistent — some programs use "I-cut" or "slot cut" for the same action.
- No claims in the body appear factually wrong.
