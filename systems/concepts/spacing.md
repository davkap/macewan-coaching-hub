# Spacing (Concept Reference)

## The rule
**Two passes away = a defender should be in the gap, not on the man.**
If your offense has good spacing, every drive collapses 2 defenders, leaving 2 open shooters.

## Spacing models

### 5-out
- All 5 above the FT line extended
- Lane is empty for cuts
- Best for skilled, switchable rosters
- See `offense/5-out.md`

### 4-out 1-in
- 4 perimeter, 1 post (dunker spot or high post)
- Best for ball-screen continuity
- See `offense/4-out-1-in-ballscreen.md`

### 3-out 2-in
- 3 perimeter, 2 bigs (high-low or twin posts)
- Best vs small lineups
- Used by traditional bruising teams

## Sacred spots
- **Corners** — every offense should have shooters in both corners as a default. Corners are the highest-EV 3 in basketball (shorter arc + most help defenders forced out).
- **Slot 45** — the area between top-of-key and wing — best driving angle
- **Dunker spot** — short corner on the strong side — for the rim-running big

## Spacing crimes
1. Two players standing in the same spot
2. A non-shooter in the corner (his defender helps freely)
3. Crowding the ball-screen (no kick-out option)
4. Crashing the offensive glass and leaving the corner empty
5. The PG drive with no exit valve

## Scout question
- Does this opponent maintain corner spacing? Or do they crash the corners and lose spacing?
- If they don't space well, we can over-help in the lane.

## Sources & further reading

### Verified citations (this file's content can be checked against)
- [Wikipedia — Three-point field goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-point_field_goal) — confirms the corner-3 distance is shorter than the above-the-break 3 (NBA: 22 ft vs. 23 ft 9 in), the structural reason corners are the highest-EV three.
- Cleaning the Glass (Ben Falk) — https://cleaningtheglass.com — published EV-by-shot-zone tables that show corner-3s as the most efficient non-rim shot in the half-court. Falk is former VP of Basketball Operations for the 76ers; some content paywalled.
- Kirk Goldsberry, *Sprawlball* (2019, HMH) — book-length treatment of how the 3-point line and corner spacing reshaped NBA offenses; chapters on the "spread floor" and gravity.

### Recommended further reading for staff
- Fastmodel Sports Playbank — search "5-out spacing", "4-out 1-in", "corner spacing" at https://fastmodelsports.com for diagrammed continuity offenses.
- Zach Lowe at *The Ringer* — repeated columns on "spacing crimes" (corner-cutting non-shooters, dunker-spot bigs). https://www.theringer.com/authors/zach-lowe.
- Dean Oliver, *Basketball on Paper* (2004) — Four Factors framework that motivates why spacing-driven efficient shots matter most.
- Seth Partnow, *The Midrange Theory* (2021, Triumph) — the analytical case for corner threes, rim attempts, and floor spacing.

### Verification notes (Claude)
- "Two passes away = gap defender" is a near-universal coaching rule (sometimes attributed to Dick Bennett's pack-line and Tom Izzo's man defense), but I did not find a single canonical citation for the exact phrasing.
- "Corners are the highest-EV 3" is well-supported in NBA data (Cleaning the Glass, Sprawlball); for college and HS the gap is smaller because the lines are closer together. Flagged: the EV gap shrinks at lower levels.
- The "3-out 2-in vs 4-out 1-in vs 5-out" taxonomy is standard coaching vocabulary but not from a single source.
- "Sacred spots" naming is the coach's own coinage; concept is sound.
