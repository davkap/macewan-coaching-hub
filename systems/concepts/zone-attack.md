# Zone Attack (Concept Reference)

How to attack any zone — 2-3, 1-3-1, match-up, 3-2.

## Universal zone-attack principles

### 1. Attack the gaps, not the players
Zone defenders cover areas. Drive into the gap **between** two defenders — you make both decide.

### 2. High-post flash
Get a passer to the FT line. The high-post player can hit cutters, short corners, or shoot. The high post is the brain of zone attack.

### 3. Short corner
The gap between the wing defender and the bottom defender. Always open. Always.

### 4. Skip passes
Zone shifts to the ball; skip passes catch defenders rotating late.

### 5. Ball reversal speed
Zones can't shift fast enough to cover speed reversal. Snap the ball side to side.

### 6. Overload
Put 3 offensive players on one side. The zone can't cover. Force them to rotate, then skip.

### 7. Don't pass back
Once you reverse the ball, don't pass back to the side you just came from. Keep the zone moving.

### 8. Offensive rebound
Zone defenders don't have men to box out. Crash hard.

### 9. Cutters through gaps
Send cutters through the seams of the zone. Forces defenders to make decisions.

## Zone-specific notes

### vs 2-3
- High post is the killer (between the 2 top guards and the 3 bottom defenders)
- Short corners are wide open
- Skip from corner to corner

### vs 1-3-1
- Short corner KILLS 1-3-1 (back defender alone)
- Skip over the top (middle defender committed)
- Offensive rebound the back defender

### vs match-up
- Ball reversal before they match up
- Cutter overloads to confuse who picks up whom

### vs 3-2
- Attack the corners (only one bottom defender per side)
- High post + ball reversal

## Set play vs zone (universal)
- **Cluster on one side, ball-reverse, skip + short-corner finish** — works against every zone

## Famous zone-attackers
- Tom Izzo (Michigan State) — relentless ball reversal vs Syracuse 2-3
- Roy Williams (Carolina) — high-low through the zone
