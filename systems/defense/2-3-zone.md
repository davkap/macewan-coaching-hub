# 2-3 Zone Defense

**Family:** zone — two players at the top of the key, three across the baseline.

## Strengths
- Protects the paint (3 bigs near the rim)
- Hides slow-footed defenders
- Disrupts unprepared offenses
- Saves legs over a long game / tournament

## Weaknesses (how to attack 2-3 zone)
- **High post / free-throw line flash** — kills the zone if a big can pass from the elbow.
- **Short corner** — the gap between bottom defenders is exposed.
- **Skip passes** — bottom of the zone shifts late; weak-side wing is open.
- **Ball-screens at the top** — drag the top guards out, big rotates, layup or short corner.
- **Overload** — put 3 offensive players on one side; zone can't cover.
- **Offensive rebounding** — zone defenders don't have specific box-out assignments.

## Famous practitioners
- Jim Boeheim (Syracuse) — the most committed 2-3 zone team in modern basketball
- Many lower-division and youth programs

## Scout signals (they're playing 2-3 if…)
- Defenders are positioned in zones (not chasing players)
- Two players at the top of the key on every possession
- Three players across the baseline
- Less switching, more "who's open in my area"

- *Bud! On Coach K, Carmelo, the Recruiting Wars, and Beyond* — Jim Boeheim with Jack McCallum (2014) — Boeheim's own account of why and how he committed to the 2-3 zone at Syracuse.
