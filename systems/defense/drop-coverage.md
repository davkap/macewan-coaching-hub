# Drop Coverage (Ball-Screen Defense)

**Family:** ball-screen defensive scheme — the most common coverage in basketball.

## How it works
- **Big drops** to the level of the rim (or short of the FT line) on the ball-screen.
- **Guard fights over the screen** and chases the ball-handler from behind.
- **Weak-side help** stays attached to shooters; tag the roller from the nail.

## Why teams use it
- Protects the rim — no easy layups
- Keeps your bigs out of foul trouble
- Simple to teach
- Forces offenses to make tough mid-range pull-ups

## Variations by depth
- **Deep drop** (at the rim) — protects against the lob, gives up pull-up
- **Soft drop** (at the FT line) — contests pull-up, gives up the lob
- **High drop / show** — big steps up, then recovers; hybrid

## Strengths
- Eliminates rim attempts
- Limits 4-on-3 chaos that comes with hedges/blitzes
- Keeps your defense organized and rebounding-ready

## Weaknesses (how to attack drop)
- **Pull-up jumpers** — elite-shooting guards (Curry, Lillard, college equivalents) feast on drop.
- **Pocket passes to the roller** in the FT-line area
- **Snake dribble** — ball-handler dribbles back into the screen, gets to mid-range
- **Re-screens** — force the big to recover twice
- **Spain PnR** — back-screen on the dropping big = lob city

## Scout signals (they're in drop if…)
- Big never leaves the paint on ball-screens
- Guard goes over the top, never under
- Pull-up jumpers are the main shot the offense gets
- Few defensive switches

## Famous practitioners
- Most NBA teams default to drop with a rim-protecting 5
- Rudy Gobert (Jazz/Wolves) — quintessential drop big
- College programs with traditional centers

### Verified citations (this file's content can be checked against)

- Cleaning the Glass (cleaningtheglass.com) — Ben Falk's site has extensive written coverage of NBA PnR coverage trends, including the 2014–2020 league-wide swing toward drop coverage with rim-protecting 5s (Gobert era Jazz, Adams-era Thunder, Allen / Mobley Cavs).

- BBALLBREAKDOWN (YouTube), Thinking Basketball (YouTube), HalfCourtHoops (YouTube) — many drop-coverage breakdown videos using Gobert, Embiid, and college-rim-protector tape; especially good on "deep drop vs soft drop" trade-offs and how Curry / Lillard / Doncic punish drop with pull-up 3s.

- FastModel Sports Playbank — search "drop coverage" and "ball screen defense" for diagrammed rules and tag responsibilities.

### Verification notes (Claude)
- "Most common coverage in basketball" is true for the late-2010s NBA and remains common, but the league has shifted noticeably toward more switching and higher coverages since ~2022 (especially in the playoffs vs elite shooting guards). Coaches should treat this file as accurate but understand the NBA mix has moved.
- The Spain PnR / back-screen counter to drop is well-documented; the file's framing ("lob city") is accurate.
