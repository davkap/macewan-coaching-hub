# ICE / Blue (Ball-Screen Defense)

**Family:** side ball-screen coverage. ICE = "Force the ball away from the screen, toward the baseline."

## How it works (on a side ball-screen)
- **Guard jumps to the high side** of the screen, denying middle penetration.
- **Big drops to the strong-side block**, sealing the baseline.
- **Weak-side defenders** rotate to the nail and tag the roller.
- Ball-handler is forced into a baseline trap or a contested mid-range.

## Why teams use it
- Protects the middle of the floor (where most help comes from)
- Predictable for the defense — everyone knows where the ball is going
- Devalues pick-and-roll vs side ball-screens

## Strengths
- Eliminates middle drives
- Funnels offense to the worst part of the floor (baseline corner)
- Easy to teach for college teams

## Weaknesses (how to attack ICE)
- **Reject the screen** — go away from the screener, attack the open middle the guard vacated.
- **Step-up screen on the other side** — flip the action so the screen is now on the open side.
- **Re-screen back to middle** — the defense has to re-set.
- **Slip the screen** — screener slips to the rim before contact; defense over-commits to ICE-ing the ball.
- **Stagger / second screen** — two screens are harder to ICE.
- **Throw to the strong-side corner shooter** — the defense often forgets him while ICE-ing.

## Scout signals (they're ICE-ing if…)
- Guards jump high on side ball-screens
- Bigs drop to the strong-side block (not the middle)
- Offense ends up trapped in the corner often
- Middle of the floor stays empty

## Famous practitioners
- Tom Thibodeau (Bulls/Knicks/Wolves) — popularized ICE in NBA
- Used by most NBA & high-level college teams as the **default side-PnR coverage**

### Verification notes (Claude)
- "Tom Thibodeau popularized ICE in NBA" is the consensus story — he was a Doc Rivers assistant on the 2008 Celtics, where ICE / "Blue" terminology became famous via Kevin Garnett's communication. Multiple oral histories credit Thibs and that Celtics staff. I could not pin a single primary source URL in this session, but the attribution is widely repeated and not in serious dispute.
- The terms "ICE" and "Blue" mean the same thing across most NBA staffs but call-outs vary by team.
- All listed counters (reject, step-up flip, re-screen, slip, stagger, strong-side corner skip) are textbook and consistent with public coaching content.
