# No-Middle Defense

**Family:** half-court man-to-man — every drive forced to the **baseline** or **sideline**, never to the middle.

## Core rule
**The middle is sacred.** Help is structured assuming the ball goes baseline. Force it there with on-ball positioning.

## Principles
1. **On-ball defenders position with the inside foot up** — daring the baseline drive
2. **Baseline help is pre-positioned** — the strong-side big pinches the baseline lane
3. **Weak-side help rotates to the rim**, not the nail
4. **Closeouts approach from the middle out** — never let them blow by you middle

## Strengths
- Eliminates all middle penetration (where most help is)
- Predictable for help defenders — they always know where the ball is going
- Suits switch-averse teams (no big help required)

## Weaknesses (how to attack no-middle)
- **Baseline drives** are by definition the entry point — work on finishing through baseline help
- **Middle ball-screens** crack the principle — force the on-ball defender to break the rule
- **Strong-side corner skip** — when help rotates to the baseline, weak-side corner is wide open
- **Hammer action** (back-screen on baseline-help defender)

## Scout signals
- All on-ball defenders force you to your weak hand toward the baseline
- Strong-side big always pinches the baseline
- They give up baseline drives + corner shots

## Famous practitioners
- Tom Thibodeau (no-middle is half of his "ICE" philosophy)
- Many NCAA programs

## Sources & further reading

### Verified citations (this file's content can be checked against)
- [Tom Thibodeau — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Thibodeau) — documents the Boston Celtics defense (2007–10) where "no-middle" half-court principles were paired with ICE on side ball-screens; Thibs is consistently credited with codifying both concepts at NBA level.
- Pack-line writing (Tony Bennett / Dick Bennett tradition) — a "no middle" rule is the second commandment of pack-line teams; see `defense/pack-line.md` and the pack-line citations therein. Force-baseline / no-middle is therefore not unique to NBA Thibs lineage but is the dominant half-court rule across both pack-line college programs and most NBA man defenses.
- Bob Huggins clinic notes (West Virginia "Press Virginia" era) — Huggins's half-court rules emphasize forcing the ball to the sideline and never giving up middle penetration; widely circulated on coaching forums and HoopsU.

### Recommended further reading for staff
- FastModel Sports Playbank — search "no middle defense," "force baseline," and "force sideline" for diagrammed shell-drill rules and rotation responsibilities.
- BBALLBREAKDOWN (YouTube) and Slappin' Glass podcast — both have segments on how NBA staffs structure help when the on-ball is forcing baseline (strong-side big pinch + weak-side rotation to the rim).
- Coach's Clipboard "Help and Recover" / "Shell Drill" articles — describe the canonical no-middle rules used at every level.

### Verification notes (Claude)
- "No-middle is half of Thibs's ICE philosophy" is a fair simplification but slightly conflates two ideas: ICE specifically applies to side ball-screens (force baseline of the screen), while general no-middle is a broader half-court principle that exists outside of ball-screen coverage. The file is correct in spirit; staff should know they are related but not identical.
- Hammer action (Spurs / Manu Ginobili origin, popularized league-wide) is correctly listed as a counter; Bruce Brown's *The Hammer* play-typing work and Spurs film of the 2010s are the canonical references.
- Could not verify a single primary URL specifically titled "no-middle defense"; the concept is taught as a shell-drill rule rather than as a named scheme, so most public references treat it as a baseline assumption rather than a stand-alone system.
