# 5-Out Motion / Modern Spread

**Family:** spaced motion with no traditional post; everyone can pass, dribble, shoot.

## Spacing
All 5 players above the free-throw line extended. Often a stretch-5 at the top of the key. Lane is open for cuts.

## Core principles
1. **Lane is empty by design.** Every cut goes downhill to a vacant rim.
2. **Dribble at = back-cut.** When a teammate dribbles toward you, you back-cut.
3. **Pass and replace** — fill the spot of whoever cut.
4. **0.5 decisions** — shoot, pass, or drive within half a second of catch.

## Signature actions
- **Dribble hand-off (DHO) chains** at the top
- **Veer screens** (back-screen on the help defender)
- **Empty-side ball-screens** — no help available
- **Chicago action** — pin-down → DHO

## Counters / answers
- **vs Pack-line:** hard to attack the rim; must shoot over it. Counter with skip passes and high-volume threes.
- **vs Switch:** PG-on-5 mismatches everywhere; attack instantly.
- **vs Drop:** PG pull-up jumper game; or drive-and-kick.

## Scout signals
- 5-out alignment with no traditional 5
- DHO at top of key on most possessions
- Lots of corner threes and weak-side cuts
- High-volume 3PA

## Famous practitioners
- Erik Spoelstra (Heat)
- Steve Nash / Jason Kidd offensive systems
- Many modern NCAA mid-majors

### Verified citations (this file's content can be checked against)
- [Motion offense — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_offense) — covers the read-and-react principles that underpin 5-out (pass-and-replace, screen away, back screens) and Bob Knight's foundational work that modern 5-out descends from.
- [Continuity offense — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_offense) — clarifies the difference between motion (free-flowing rules-based) and continuity (patterned), useful when teaching the "0.5" decision framework.
- *The Read & React Offense* — Rick Torbett (Better Basketball) — the most thorough public articulation of dribble-at / back-cut and pass-and-fill rules used in modern 5-out.

### Verification notes (Claude)
- "Chicago action" (pin-down into DHO) is a real, widely used term; not formally documented on Wikipedia but heavily covered on FastModel and BBALLBREAKDOWN.
- Attribution of 5-out specifically to Spoelstra, Nash, or Kidd is reasonable but loose — none of those coaches "invented" 5-out, they just leaned into 5-out lineups (Heat with Bam Adebayo as the lone "5," etc.). The Warriors and Villanova are arguably more iconic 5-out / split-action programs and could be added.
- The "0.5 second" decision rule is associated with Del Harris / Spurs coaching tree; not specifically tied to 5-out as a system.
