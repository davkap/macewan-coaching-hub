# Dribble-Drive Motion (Vance Walberg → John Calipari)

**Family:** spread offense built around penetrate-and-kick. No traditional post. Lane is open by design.

## Spacing
**4-out 1-in** with the 5 in the **dunker spot** (short corner block). Four perimeter players outside the arc; the lane is empty for drives.

## Core principles
1. **Drives are the engine.** Every possession should generate a paint touch.
2. **Drift on the strong side, fill behind on the weak side.** When the ball is driven, the strong-side corner drifts deeper; weak-side wing fills the slot.
3. **Kick to the open shooter, then re-attack.** Drive-and-kick triggers another drive from the catch.
4. **The dunker doesn't crash early.** He stays under so the rim is open for the drive.

## Signature actions
- **Penetrate to the rack** — first option on every catch
- **Kick out to corner** — when help comes
- **Drift + 45 cut** — strong-side wing drifts, weak-side wing cuts to the slot
- **Dribble at = back-cut** (shared with 5-out)

## Counters / answers
- **vs Pack-line:** harder — the help is already in the lane. Counter with skip passes and the dunker sealing inside.
- **vs Switch:** drive at the slowest perimeter defender, attack the switched mismatch.
- **vs Drop:** PG attacks the drop big in space — pull-up or finish.

## Scout signals (you're seeing dribble-drive if…)
- 4-out spacing with one big in the dunker spot
- Almost every possession starts with a drive (no called set)
- Lots of corner 3s and rim attempts; few mid-range
- High pace

## Famous practitioners
- Vance Walberg (originator)
- John Calipari (Memphis / Kentucky)
- Many high-school programs

## Sources & further reading

### Verified citations (this file's content can be checked against)
- [Dribble drive motion — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dribble_drive_motion) — confirms Walberg as originator (named "AASAA" — Attack-Attack-Skip-Attack-Attack) at Clovis West HS in 1997, refined at Fresno City College (2002–2006); Calipari adopted it at Memphis for 2005–06 after a 2003 dinner with Walberg; describes the 4-out / one-in-the-dunker spacing and the read-based (not play-based) decision tree exactly as in this file.
- [Vance Walberg — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vance_Walberg) — verifies Walberg's coaching path (Clovis West → Fresno CC → Pepperdine → UMass → Nuggets → 76ers → Kings → Clovis West → Fresno State 2024–) and his authorship of DDM.
- *Vance Walberg's Dribble Drive Motion Offense* — Vance Walberg & John Calipari (Championship Productions instructional DVDs) — primary source from the originator and his most famous adopter.

- Grant Wahl, "Just Drive, Baby" — *Sports Illustrated*, 2008 — feature on Walberg, Calipari, and the spread of DDM (referenced as the SI source on the Wikipedia page).
- Mike DeCourcy, *The Sporting News* — multiple pieces on the "Memphis Attack" during the 2007–08 Memphis Final Four run.
- BBALLBREAKDOWN and Coach Daniel (YouTube) — film breakdowns of Calipari Kentucky DDM and the 4-out spread spacing.

### Verification notes (Claude)
- Origin story (Walberg → Calipari, 1997 / 2003 dinner / 2005–06 Memphis) is solidly documented.
- The "224 teams by 2007–08" figure on Wikipedia is sourced to the Wahl SI piece and is era-specific — adoption is much wider now.
- "Few mid-range" is more a characteristic of *modern* analytics-driven DDM than Walberg's original version, which did include mid-range pull-ups off the drive. Worth flagging if presenting to purists.
