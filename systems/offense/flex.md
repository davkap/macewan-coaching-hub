# Flex Offense

**Family:** continuity offense built on the **flex cut** (baseline cut off a back-screen) and a **down-screen** for the screener.

**Origin:** invented by **Rene Herrerias at Cal-Berkeley in 1967** (originally called the "Cha Cha"). Popularized by Gary Williams' 2002 NCAA-champion Maryland team and Jerry Sloan's Utah Jazz. Common HS/college teaching offense because it builds skill habits in cutting, screening, and reading.

## Spacing
2 above (PG + wing), 2 in the corners, 1 in the high post. Tight, screen-heavy.

## Core principles
1. **Flex cut every possession** — corner player cuts off baseline back-screen from the post → layup or fill the lane.
2. **Screen the screener** — the back-screener gets a down-screen and pops to the wing.
3. **Continuity** — the action repeats from the other side.

## Signature actions
- **Flex cut** → **down-screen for the cutter** → ball reversal → repeat
- **Flex with a ball-screen counter** when defenders cheat the cut

## Counters / answers (when YOU run flex)
- **vs Switch-everything:** big screening for guard creates mismatch on the cut.
- **vs Pack-line:** flex cut hits the rim through the soft middle.
- **vs Zone:** modify with high-post flash, but flex isn't ideal vs zone — have a zone-attack call ready.

## Scout signals (you're seeing flex if…)
- Repeated baseline cuts off post-player back-screens
- Same pattern run from both sides of the floor
- Lots of layups and short-corner jumpers
- Methodical pace (15+ second possessions)

## How to defend flex
- **Top-lock the flex cutter** so they can't curl off the screen
- **Switch the back-screen + the down-screen** to kill continuity
- Force them out of pattern — pressure the entry pass
- Keep your big at the rim; they want layups

## Famous practitioners
- **Rene Herrerias** (Cal-Berkeley, 1967) — originator
- **Gary Williams** (Maryland) — 2002 NCAA championship
- **Jerry Sloan** (Utah Jazz) — long-running NBA flex variant
- **Al Skinner** (Boston College) — well-documented college flex era
- Many high school and college programs (great for skill-development teaching)

### Verification notes (Claude)
- **Origin attribution in the file's title hints (Carl Tacy / Wake Forest) is incorrect.** The verifiable origin is Rene Herrerias at Cal-Berkeley, 1967. Carl Tacy at Wake Forest (1972–1985) ran flex variants but did not invent it. The coach should consider correcting any reference to Tacy as originator.
- "Riley-era Heat" running flex is **not well-supported.** Sloan/Jazz is solidly documented; Riley's Heat were more known for post-up isolation and triangle-influenced sets. Boston College under Al Skinner is a much better-documented NBA-adjacent flex example.
- Gary Williams / Maryland 2002 title is confirmed.
- The 90s "NBA staple" framing is a stretch — flex was more common in college and high school than in the NBA, where Sloan's Jazz were the notable exception.
