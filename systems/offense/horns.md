# Horns Set Family

**Family:** set-play family (not a system) — extremely common in college and pro. Two bigs at the elbows, two shooters in the corners, PG up top.

## Alignment
```
        PG
   B         B    (elbows)


   S         S    (corners)
```

## Why coaches love it
- Forces the defense to declare its ball-screen coverage immediately
- Multiple counters from the same look (defense can't tell which is coming)
- Spacing is perfect: corners + elbows + top

## Signature variations
- **Horns:** double drag ball-screen — PG attacks middle
- **Horns flare:** weak-side big back-screens for ball-side big who flares to the wing
- **Horns down:** PG enters to elbow → down-screen for opposite corner shooter
- **Horns twist:** ball-screen + slip by second screener
- **Horns chest:** PG dribbles off one elbow → other elbow steps up for second ball-screen
- **Horns Spain:** add a back-screen on the dropping big's defender

## Counters / answers (running Horns)
- **vs Drop:** pull-up jumper or roll lob
- **vs Switch:** attack mismatch immediately
- **vs Blitz:** short-roll big at the elbow, 4-on-3
- **vs Hedge:** snake dribble back to middle

## Scout signals
- Two-big elbow alignment after made baskets
- Multiple actions out of the same alignment
- Heavy use late in shot clock or out of timeouts

## How to defend Horns
- Pre-call the coverage at the elbow so both bigs know the plan
- Top-lock the corner shooters so they can't lift
- Switch the second action if it's a screen-the-screener look

### Verification notes (Claude)
- The Horns origin is genuinely contested — it is widely credited to European basketball but no single inventor is documented; the file wisely avoids naming one. I could not find a single authoritative "first Horns set" citation.
- All variation names (Flare, Down, Twist, Chest, Spain) are standard coaching vocabulary and appear across FastModel/HoopsU/coaching clinics, but specific naming conventions vary by program.
- "Spain" is well-documented as originating with the Spanish national team's back-screen-the-roller action; the Horns Spain hybrid is a modern combination.
