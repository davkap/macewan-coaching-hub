# Motion Offense (Bob Knight / Indiana lineage)

**Family:** read-and-react continuity. No called plays — players make decisions based on rules and reads.

## Spacing
4-out-1-in or 5-out depending on personnel. Big at high post or short corner.

## Core principles
1. **Pass and screen away** — after every pass, screen for the next-closest non-ball teammate.
2. **Pass and cut** — alternative: cut hard to the rim, replace from the weak side.
3. **Screen the screener** — when a screen is set, the next action is to screen for that screener.
4. **Read the defense, not the diagram.**

## Required reads
- **On-ball:** drive lane → drive; help comes → kick to skip or short corner.
- **Off-ball cutter:** if defender turns head → backdoor; if defender plays high → curl.
- **Screener:** slip if defender hedges; pop if defender drops; roll if defender switches late.

## Counters / answers
- **vs Pack-line:** stretch the floor with shooters; use back screens to attack the gaps; flash to high post for ball reversal.
- **vs Switch-everything:** screen with mismatched bodies, attack the switch immediately (post-up or drive-by).
- **vs 2-3 zone:** ball-screen the top guard, drag the zone, hit the short corner / high post.
- **vs 1-3-1:** flash to free-throw line, attack baseline gaps, skip-pass over the trap.

## Scout signals (you're seeing motion if…)
- No discernible called sets after made baskets
- Constant screening away from the ball
- Players cutting hard after every pass
- Bigs flashing high-low

## Modern hybrids
- **Princeton motion** (cuts + chin action + back-cuts; see `princeton.md`)
- **Read-and-react** (Rick Torbett system — layered reactions)
- **5-out motion** (modern NBA — replaces traditional post motion)

## Sources & further reading

### Verified citations (this file's content can be checked against)
- [Wikipedia — Motion offense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_offense) — confirms Bob Knight's role developing the modern motion offense at Indiana, his influence from Pete Newell and the Princeton offense (after the 1972 Olympic trials), and the rules-based (not play-call) structure described in this file.
- [Jeff Walker, "Knight Developed Motion Offense Through Research, And He's Always Adapting" (Avalanche-Journal, archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150127225035/http://www.knight880.com/special/stories/motion.shtml) — primary source on Knight's evolution from Army's "reverse action" to the Indiana motion.
- [FastModel Sports Playbank — Motion Offense tag](https://fastmodel.com/playbank?tag=motion-offense) — diagrammed examples of pass-and-screen-away, pass-and-cut, screen-the-screener actions matching the rules in this file.
- Dean Smith, *Basketball: Multiple Offense and Defense* (Allyn & Bacon, 1981) — UNC's "passing game" variant, a major influence on modern motion concepts (cited in most motion-offense literature).
- Hubie Brown clinic talks (available through Coaching U / Championship Productions) — rules-based motion teaching from a Hall of Fame source.
