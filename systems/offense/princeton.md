# Princeton Offense (Pete Carril)

**Family:** continuity motion with heavy use of back-cuts, ball reversal, and high-post passing.

## Spacing
2 guards above the arc, 2 wings, 1 high post (the **chin**). Floor is balanced and high.

## Core principles
1. **Back-cut on overplay.** If your defender denies the pass, cut hard to the rim. Punishes pressure.
2. **High-post is the brain.** Ball goes to the chin (5 or stretch 4) frequently — high-post entry triggers cuts.
3. **Ball reversal forces help rotation.** Constant side-to-side movement to find the gap.
4. **Pace is deliberate.** Princeton thrives in half-court grind games; uses clock.

## Signature actions
- **Chin series:** pass to high post → backdoor cut from the weak-side wing.
- **Point series:** point guard drives + hands off to wing curling around.
- **Low series:** entry to low post → splits / ball-screen at top.
- **UCLA cut into back screen for the chin.**

## Counters / answers vs different defenses
- **vs Pack-line:** Princeton EATS pack-line because back-cuts attack the sagging defenders. Pack-line teams must close out hard, which opens the back-door.
- **vs Switch-everything:** harder — switches eliminate the back-cut window. Counter with on-ball ball-screens and post-ups vs mismatches.
- **vs Zone:** flash to the chin, hit short corner, attack high post-low post seam.

## Scout signals (you're seeing Princeton if…)
- Frequent passes to a high-post player, often a stretch-5
- Wings constantly back-cutting on dribble penetration to top
- Lots of dribble hand-offs at the elbow
- Possessions take 18+ seconds; deliberate

## Famous practitioners
- Pete Carril (Princeton)
- Bill Carmody (Northwestern)
- John Thompson III (Georgetown)
- Mitch Henderson (Princeton) — modern continuation
- Common ACC influence (Carmody, JT3) and a recurring tempo-control philosophy at the small-college and HS level

### Verification notes (Claude)
- "Pete Carril (originator)" — slightly imprecise. Wikipedia traces Princeton-style concepts to Cappy Cappon and Red Sarachek decades earlier; Carril *perfected and popularized* the system rather than inventing it. The file says "Pete Carril (Princeton)" which is fine.
- All four named series (Chin, Point, Low, UCLA) are documented standard Princeton terminology.
- The "Princeton EATS pack-line" claim is widely held coaching wisdom and logically sound (back-cuts attack sagging defenders) but I could not find a single statistical study proving it; treat as expert consensus, not data-verified.
