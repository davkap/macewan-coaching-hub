# Read-and-React (Rick Torbett)

**Family:** layered motion / decision-tree system. Replaces called plays with 20+ "layers" of conditioned reactions to teammate actions.

## Core philosophy
Every player reacts to what their **teammates** do, not what they call. If a teammate dribbles at you, you back-cut. If a teammate passes, you fill. The system runs itself.

## The layers (sample — there are ~20 total)
1. **Pass and cut** — every pass triggers a basket cut
2. **Circle movement** — fill the spot vacated
3. **Post pin and skip** — when the ball goes to the post, weak-side pins
4. **Laker cut** — pass to the post + cut over the top
5. **Dribble at = back-cut**
6. **Power dribble = post slides through to the other side**
7. **Speed dribble = perimeter players rotate spots**
... etc.

## Why coaches like it
- Teaches habits, not plays — players develop true basketball IQ
- Adapts to any personnel
- Killer teaching tool for developmental programs

## Counters / answers
- The system is the counter — every defensive adjustment triggers a different layer

## Scout signals (you're seeing R&R if…)
- Constant cutting and filling, no obvious set
- Players react identically to the same triggers (recognizable to a trained eye)
- No "play calls" from the bench during half-court possessions

## Famous practitioners
- Rick Torbett (originator)
- Many high-school and college developmental programs
