# Triangle Offense (Tex Winter)

**Family:** continuity / read-and-react half-court system organized around a strong-side triangle (corner, wing, post).

## Spacing
- Strong-side **triangle:** corner, wing, post
- Weak-side **two-man game:** point + weak-side wing in a "two-man game" up top

## Core principles
1. **Strong-side triangle creates passing angles** — every defender has to cover two players.
2. **Read the post entry.** Most actions trigger off a pass to the strong-side post.
3. **No called plays.** Players make decisions from a set of options based on defensive positioning.
4. **Sideline-triangle vs middle entries** — different reads from each.

## Signature actions
- **Post entry → backdoor cut** by the wing if the defender helps
- **Post entry → pinch post** for the weak-side player
- **Two-man game → guard-to-guard pass + back-screen**

## Counters / answers
- **vs Hard double-team in the post:** pass back to the wing for a skip to the weak-side
- **vs Switching:** triangle generates mismatches in the post almost every possession

## Scout signals
- Strong-side triangle alignment after entry
- Frequent post touches (high or low)
- Methodical pace
- Heavy reads-based, no obvious play calls

## Famous practitioners
- Tex Winter (originator)
- Phil Jackson (Bulls 6 titles, Lakers 5 titles) — Kobe and MJ won everything inside this offense
- Few teams run pure triangle today, but its concepts (post-entry reads, weak-side action) live on

## Sources & further reading

### Verified citations (this file's content can be checked against)
- [Wikipedia — Triangle offense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_offense) — confirms the strong-side triangle structure (corner, wing, post) + weak-side two-man game, the post-entry-driven reads, the "every pass and cut has a purpose" philosophy. Notes the system's basic ideas were originally established by Hall of Fame coach Sam Barry at USC and then developed by Tex Winter, Barry's player.
- [Wikipedia — Tex Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tex_Winter) — biographical confirmation: 9× NBA champion as Phil Jackson's assistant (Bulls 1991–93, 1996–98; Lakers 2000–02), Hall of Fame 2011, Chuck Daly Lifetime Achievement Award 2010.
- Tex Winter, *The Triple-Post Offense* (Prentice-Hall, 1962) — the canonical primary text for triangle principles; long out of print but still the most cited written source for the system.
- [BBALLBREAKDOWN — Triangle Offense breakdowns (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=bballbreakdown+triangle+offense) — film walkthroughs of Bulls/Lakers triangle execution.

### Recommended further reading for staff
- Phil Jackson & Hugh Delehanty, *Sacred Hoops* (Hyperion, 1995) and *Eleven Rings* (Penguin, 2013) — Jackson's accounts of installing the triangle with Jordan/Pippen and later Kobe/Shaq.
- Roland Lazenby, *Mindgames: Phil Jackson's Long Strange Journey* (Contemporary Books, 2001) — cultural and strategic history of the triangle's championship runs.
- [Thinking Basketball / Ben Taylor (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/@ThinkingBasketball) — modern analytical takes on why pure triangle offense is rare today (poor fit with the 3-point era and ball-handler-driven NBA).

### Verification notes (Claude)
- "Tex Winter (originator)" — slightly imprecise. Wikipedia and triangle-offense histories credit Sam Barry (USC) with the foundational concepts; Tex Winter (a Barry player) developed and codified the modern system. Winter is correctly considered the system's foremost teacher and the person who carried it to championship-level success.
- "Bulls 6 titles, Lakers 5 titles" — verified (Phil Jackson's 11 rings: 1991–93, 1996–98 Bulls; 2000–02, 2009–10 Lakers).
- "Kobe and MJ won everything inside this offense" — accurate at a high level; both stars also won outside the triangle in select non-Jackson years (e.g., Kobe was coached without triangle pre-1999 and post-2011), but their championship-era basketball was triangle-based.
- No major suspect claims; the file is conceptually solid. Origin attribution is the only nuance to flag.
