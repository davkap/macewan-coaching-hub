# BLOB / SLOB / ATO Playbook (Special Situations)

## Definitions
- **BLOB** — Baseline Out of Bounds (under our basket)
- **SLOB** — Sideline Out of Bounds
- **ATO** — After Time-Out (out of dead ball, full set)

## Why these matter
Special situations are 5–10% of possessions but 15–20% of margin in close games. Coaches who win the SS battle win more 1-possession games.

## BLOB principles
- **Get a layup or a 3.** Mid-range BLOBs are wasted possessions.
- **Stack alignment** is the most common (4 players in a line at the FT line).
- **Box alignment** is best vs zone.
- Have at least 3 BLOBs: one vs man-switch, one vs man-no-switch, one vs zone.

## Common BLOB sets
- **Stack — back-screen for shooter** flares to corner
- **Box — high-low** to the big posting up
- **Inbounder cut** — passer steps in and screens for himself off a stack

## SLOB principles
- Need a scoring SLOB AND a safe-entry SLOB (just get the ball in)
- Prefer ball-screens for the inbounder catching the ball
- "Hammer" action (back-screen for the corner) is a SLOB staple

## ATO principles
- Defense expects something — counter their expectation
- Best ATOs run from a non-traditional alignment to force adjustments
- Have ATOs by score situation: down 3, down 1, tied, up 2

## How to defend BLOB / SLOB / ATO
- **Identify their go-to** — every team has 1-2 they trust in close games. Scout it.
- **Pre-call coverage** in the huddle
- **Top-lock their best shooter** — make him the inbounder if possible
- **Switch all screens** on inbound plays unless game plan says otherwise
- **Box out** — many BLOB plays end in tip-ins

## Scout requirements
For every opponent, the agent should answer:
1. What is their go-to BLOB late game?
2. What is their go-to SLOB late game?
3. What is their favorite ATO out of dead-ball timeouts?
4. Who is the inbounder? Who is the safety valve?
