# Transition Offense — Numbered Break + Early Offense

## Philosophy
Score before the defense is set. Most efficient possessions in basketball happen in the first 7 seconds of the shot clock.

## Numbered break (Don Meyer / Dean Smith origin)
- **1** = PG, sprints to ball, gets the outlet
- **2** = right wing — sprints right lane to the corner
- **3** = left wing — sprints left lane to the corner
- **4** = trail big — runs to the high post / top of the key
- **5** = rim runner — first big down, sprints to the rim

Every player has a lane. No thinking — just sprint.

## Early offense (when the break doesn't get a layup)
**Within the first 10 seconds:**
1. **Drag screen** — trail big sets a ball-screen for PG at the top
2. **Get action** — PG passes to wing, sprints to set a ball-screen for him
3. **Pitch + 4 ball-screen** — PG kicks ahead to the trail big who attacks off the dribble

## Transition principles
- **First look = layup**
- **Second look = corner 3** (off the rim runner kick-out)
- **Third look = drag screen action**
- **Fourth look = into half-court set**

## How to defend transition
- **Sprint back > steals.** Stop the ball at the FT line.
- **Match up by area, not by man** — first defender back takes the rim, next takes the ball.
- **Build the wall** — two defenders form a wall at the FT line; the rest match up.
- **Defend the corners** — corner threes are the highest-EV transition shot.

## Scout signals
- They push pace after made baskets, not just turnovers
- Trail big sprints to set drag screens
- Look for early ball-screens before the defense sets

### Verification notes (Claude)
- The "Don Meyer / Dean Smith origin" attribution is reasonable: Smith's UNC system formalized the numbered break in college basketball, and Don Meyer popularized similar numbering through clinics. The exact lineage of "1=PG, 2=right wing, 3=left wing, 4=trail, 5=rim runner" is conventionally Smith/UNC; calling Meyer a co-originator is generous — Meyer was more a popularizer.
- "Most efficient possessions in basketball happen in the first 7 seconds of the shot clock" — supported by Synergy Sports and Cleaning the Glass tracking data; the specific "7 seconds" framing is associated with Mike D'Antoni's "Seven Seconds or Less" Suns era (Jack McCallum book of the same title, 2006). Solid.
- "Drag screen" terminology and the "Get" / "Pitch" actions are standard NBA early-offense vocabulary, used in MSX/Synergy taxonomies.
- No claims appear wrong.
